42. A PAN is not a random string; every character has a specific meaning. The format is always 5 Letters + 4 Numbers + 1 Letter.
43. Position  (Col 2: `Type` | Col 3: `Meaning` | Col 4: `Example (ABCPS1234F)`)
44. 1 - 3  (Col 2: `Letters` | Col 3: `A random alphabetic series (AAA to ZZZ).` | Col 4: `ABC`)
45. 4.0  (Col 2: `Letter` | Col 3: `The Status of the Holder. This is the most critical digit.` | Col 4: `P (Individual)`)
46. 5.0  (Col 2: `Letter` | Col 3: `The Surname/Name. The first letter of the person's last name or the entity's name.` | Col 4: `S (e.g., Sharma)`)
47. 6 - 9  (Col 2: `Numbers` | Col 3: `A sequential series of digits (0001 to 9999).` | Col 4: `1234.0`)
48. 10.0  (Col 2: `Letter` | Col 3: `Check Digit. An alphabetic digit used for verification.` | Col 4: `F`)
49. The 4th character tells the government what kind of "legal person" you are. Common examples include:
50. P: Individual (Person)
51. C: Company
52. H: Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)
53. F: Firm / Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)
54. T: Trust
55. G: Government Agency
56. J: Artificial Juridical Person
